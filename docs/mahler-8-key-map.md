# Mahler 8 — Key-Area Map of Part II

A guided tour of the harmonic landscape of Part II — from E-flat minor (the earthbound mountain) to E-flat major (the triumph of the Eternal Feminine), with E major as the symbolic breakthrough key.

> **Scope note:** this file covers only Part II. **Part I has no solo roles** — the Latin *Veni Creator Spiritus* hymn is sung entirely by the two mixed choirs + children's choir + orchestra. All 8 solo roles (Pater Ecstaticus, Pater Profundus, Doctor Marianus, the three penitent women, Gretchen, and the voiceless Mater Gloriosa) appear only in Part II. The collective/individual split is deliberate: Part I = divine (no individual voice); Part II = human (individual souls). Part I's key scheme is covered in `mahler-8-analysis.md §7`.

---

## 1. Why Key Symbolism Matters Here

Mahler was not a Schoenberg or a Wagner-theorist in his public statements, but his late symphonies are densely coded with **key-color associations**. In Part II specifically, three keys carry clear symbolic weight:

| Key | Symbolic function |
|---|---|
| **E-flat minor** | Earth, darkness, the mountain wilderness — the *Bergschluchten* |
| **E-flat major** | The divine, the perfected, the final home — heaven |
| **E major** | The "sudden upward impulse" — both *Accende lumen sensibus* in Part I and the Mater Gloriosa's entrance in Part II |

Part II's overall harmonic arc is therefore:

> **E-flat minor → (long modulation through the spiritual hierarchy) → E-flat major**, with **E major** reserved for moments of vertical breakthrough (the Mater Gloriosa's arrival and Doctor Marianus's address to her).

## 2. Quick Recap: Part I Key Areas

For contrast with Part II, here is the part-I map (covered in `mahler-8-analysis.md`):

| Section | Key |
|---|---|
| Exposition — first main theme (mm. 1–45) | E-flat major |
| Secondary section (mm. 46–107) | D-flat → A-flat major |
| Closing section (mm. 141–168) | "Infirma nostri corporis" — a shadow-variant of the Veni theme |
| Development second section (mm. 217–257) | C-sharp minor (Veni theme variant) → F major at *"lumen"* |
| Development third section (mm. 262–289) | **E major fortissimo** — the *Accende* breakthrough |
| Recapitulation | E-flat major |
| Coda | E-flat major, ending with offstage brass |

## 3. Part II Key-Area Map (Section by Section)

> Measure numbers are taken from the standard Mahler score (Universal Edition / Dover reprint). The mm. numbering used in the OCR'd source chapter follows the same conventions.

### 3.1 Instrumental Arioso and Patres Ecstaticus + Profundus (mm. 1–384)

| Subsection | Mm. | Key | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Orchestral prelude (Poco Adagio) | 1–166 | **E-flat minor** | Mountain landscape; cosmic silence |
| Four-line chorale (instrumental version of the choir of younger angels — *Nebelnd um Felsenhöh*) | ~580–593 | E-flat minor → relative major | Atmospheric, the mist imagery |
| Wieder langsam (foundation of the choir echoes) | ~361–384 | E-flat minor | Returns to the depths before the ecstasy |
| Pater Ecstaticus (sehr feurig, leidenschaftlich) | ~166–261 | **E-flat major** | Spiritual warmth and ecstasy |
| Hymnlike transition (motivic material from Pater Ecstaticus) | ~262–362 | (modulatory) | |
| Allegro: Intonation of the love theme (trumpet) | ~361–384 | (modulatory) | Love-theme foreshadowing |
| Pater Profundus (Allegro appassionato) | ~166–362 | **E-flat minor** | The deep earth's bass register; "so ist es die allmächtige Liebe…" |

The two patres occupy **opposite tonal poles**: Ecstaticus in E-flat major (the heights), Profundus in E-flat minor (the depths). Both are bound to the love theme.

### 3.2 Gerettet ist das edle Glied (mm. 385–420)

Borrowed directly from Part I development section 3 (mm. 262–281). Key: **E-flat major**, mirroring the *Accende* breakthrough. The angelic choir comments on the Latin prayer of Part I.

### 3.3 Rose Chorus / Scherzo (mm. 443–519)

Choir of the younger angels: *Jene Rosen, aus den Händen liebend-heiliger Büßerinnen*. Marked *Scherzando*. Key: **E-flat major**.

### 3.4 Intermezzo and More Perfected Angels (mm. 520–611)

- mm. 520–539: Intermezzo — love theme on trumpets; modulatory.
- mm. 540–580: Introduction + choir of more perfected angels — *"Uns bleibt ein Erdenrest…"*. Key: borrowing from Part I (mm. 135–168) — the *Infirma* territory; essentially the **dark-eroded relative regions** of E-flat minor / F minor.
- mm. 580–611: Chorale (glockenspiel entry); Doctor Marianus enters at m. 604, accompanying.

### 3.5 Choir of the Blessed Young Boys (mm. 612–638)

*Freudig empfangen wir / Diesen im Puppenstand.* This is **Faust's spiritual reincarnation** — the moment the soul enters the angelic state. The musical material here also appears at mm. 1187–1212 (the second boys' choir entry). Key: **E-flat major** with strong emphasis on the bright triad. The trumpet-and-horn entry at mm. 422–425 (before any text) foreshadows this in the same key.

### 3.6 Doctor Marianus — Praise of the Mater Gloriosa (mm. 639–757)

*"Höchste Herrscherin der Welt!"* — "Highest ruler of the world!"

| Subsection | Mm. | Key |
|---|---|---|
| Doctor Marianus's address | 639–641 | **E major** (the breakthrough key) |
| Continuation | 641–703 | E major with modulations |
| *"Wenn du uns befriedest"* ("when you pacify us") | 704ff. | **E-flat major** (the moment of alleviation) |

This modulation **E major → E-flat major** at the word *"befriedest"* is one of the most carefully coded moments in the score. Mahler marks it explicitly: the **sudden alleviation of the glowing fire** corresponds to the move from the breakthrough key (E major) to the home key (E-flat major). E major = breakthrough / vertical impulse; E-flat major = rest / resolution.

### 3.7 Poco più mosso — Intermezzo (mm. 758–779)

Modulation from E-flat major to E major. Preparation for the Mater Gloriosa's appearance.

### 3.8 Mater Gloriosa floating on high (mm. 780–867)

*"Äußerst langsam. Adagissimo."*

Key: **E major** (the breakthrough key, now extended into a whole section), modulating to **B major** (the dominant of E major — closer to heaven).

### 3.9 The Repentant Women — Trio (mm. 868–1079)

Three sequential song-styles (not hymn or chorale), each in different keys:

| Voice | Mm. | Starting key | Closing key |
|---|---|---|---|
| Magna Peccatrix (Mary Magdalene) | 868–905 | B major | E-flat major |
| Mulier Samaritana | 906–956 | E-flat minor | E-flat major |
| Maria Aegyptiaca | 957–992 | G minor | E-flat major |
| **Trio** (all three) | 993–1141 | (combination; includes C major, A major, A minor) | E-flat major |

Notice the **rising key pattern**: each soloist opens deeper / darker and closes in E-flat major. The three women **arrive** home.

Their music is **bracketed by the Mater Gloriosa theme**: it opens (mm. 868–871) and closes (mm. 1005–1012) with her theme. Within the Trio, the **Rose chorus theme** returns (mm. 970–978 ↔ 474–482; mm. 1058–1079 ↔ 443–461) — so the women's music is double-bounded: by Mater Gloriosa (the divine feminine ideal) and by the Rose chorus (the angelic feminine servants).

### 3.10 Gretchen / Una Poenitentium (mm. 1142–1186)

*"Er ahnet kaum das frische Leben…"*

Key: **D major** (a brighter, more pastoral mode than the surrounding passages), with **A major** as a secondary center.

The **Mater Gloriosa theme** appears here in D major — the same theme, transposed to a different mode, as Gretchen echoes the divine love she embodies.

### 3.11 Choir of the Blessed Young Boys (return, mm. 1187–1212)

Same music as mm. 613–638. The same E-flat major breakthrough that marked Faust's reincarnation now returns at the moment his soul is fully ascended.

### 3.12 Gretchen's Prayer and the Love Theme (mm. 1213–1248)

Gretchen's continuation (borrowing from Part I, mm. 46–61, 108–122: the *"Imple superna gratia"* music) in **E-flat major / A-flat major** regions. Then the love theme treated imitatively by horns and trumpets (mm. 1243–1248) — modulating toward E-flat major for the Mater Gloriosa's re-entry.

### 3.13 Mater Gloriosa dolcissimo (mm. 1249–1276)

*"Sehr langsam."* Key: **E-flat major** — she has returned to the home key. This is the second, quieter appearance of the Mater Gloriosa theme, now in its "settled" form.

### 3.14 Doctor Marianus and Choir — Hymn (mm. 1277–1383)

Key: **E-flat major** — full diatonic home.

### 3.15 Postlude — *Blicket auf!* (mm. 1384–1420)

Doctor Marianus's *Blicket auf* (Look up!) motif, copied upward. Key: **E-flat major**, with glockenspiel and tam-tam. The instructions read *diminuendo bis zum Verlöschen* — fading to extinction. A remarkable scoring effect.

### 3.16 Transition (mm. 1421–1448)

Slowly, with harmonium, celesta, piano, and harps. This is **the bridge** from the Doctor Marianus music to the Chorus Mysticus. Harmonically: preparation of the home key.

### 3.17 Chorus Mysticus (mm. 1449–1528)

Beginning very slowly.

- **Opening (*piano-pianissimo*)**: **E-flat major** — sustained chords, unaccompanied.
- **Building**: harps, organ, soaring strings layer in. The harmonic rhythm accelerates.
- **At "Das Ewig-Weibliche"**: full climax in **E-flat major**.

The Chorus Mysticus is **monotonally in E-flat major** — Mahler wants the home key fully possessed at the moment of resolution.

### 3.18 Postlude (mm. 1528–1572)

The **Veni theme from Part I** in the brass, at times in imitation. Key: **E-flat major**, with the offstage brass cadencing on the tonic.

The symphony ends, as it began, on E-flat major. The arc is closed.

## 5. The Three Keys in Detail

### 5.1 E-flat minor — the Earth / the Bergschluchten

The key of Part II's opening — a **vast, eerie landscape** of rugged mountains, thick forests, isolated hermitages. Quiet woodwind passages, muted strings, mystery and cosmic depth. Pater Profundus sings in E-flat minor: the deep, lower depths of nature.

E-flat minor in Mahler is the **key of inwardness and earthbound suffering** — compare its appearances in *Das Lied von der Erde* ("Der Abschied" begins in a deeply meditative mode) and in his song cycle *Kindertotenlieder*. It is **not** the key of heroism (that's E-flat major).

### 5.2 E-flat major — Heaven / Home

The key of Part I's opening and Part II's resolution. The basic key of the symphony.

E-flat major in Mahler is **the key of affirmation, completed work, the perfected divine** — but also the key of the *"worldly" Salzburg / Vienna* (the *Salzburg* milieu of Mahler's conducting career). The pairing of E-flat minor (heaven's nightside) and E-flat major (heaven's dayside) creates Part II's overall trajectory.

### 5.3 E major — the "Sudden Upward Impulse"

This is the most precisely coded key in the score.

E major appears in Part I at **mm. 262–289** — the development's third section, the *Mit plötzlichem Aufschwung* (With sudden impetus), where soloists + choirs + boys' choir sing *Accende lumen sensibus / Infunde amorem cordibus* in a **radiant E major fortissimo**. This is the Accende breakthrough — the only moment in Part I where the music decisively leaves E-flat territory.

In Part II, E major returns at:

1. **m. 639** — Doctor Marianus addresses the Mater Gloriosa (*"Höchste Herrscherin der Welt!"*) — the music suddenly arrives in E major with the first harp chord.
2. **mm. 780–867** — the Mater Gloriosa's entrance and floating, *Äußerst langsam / Adagissimo*. Key: E major → B major. The breakthrough is sustained.

The relationship is unmistakable:

> **E major = the key of the divine feminine at the moment of arrival / breakthrough.**
> **E-flat major = the home key where this breakthrough is then received and integrated.**

This is the same logic as Doctor Marianus's modulation at *"Wenn du uns befriedest"* — E major (the breakthrough) modulating to E-flat major (the resolution). The divine arrives; the home opens to receive it.

## 6. Why This Matters for Listening

A listener tracking the harmonic journey will notice:

1. **The mountain opening** feels oppressive not just because of texture but because of E-flat minor.
2. **The Mater Gloriosa's entrance** has an unmistakable "lift" because it is in E major — the same key as the Accende breakthrough.
3. **The Chorus Mysticus** sounds like arrival because it is in E-flat major, the home key, fully possessed.
4. **The closing Veni theme in the brass** is not just thematic but harmonic — the symphony returns to its opening key with the opening motif.

The Eighth is therefore **harmonically arch-shaped**: E-flat minor (Part II opening) → E major (Mater Gloriosa's breakthrough) → E-flat major (Chorus Mysticus). All the intervening keys are either approaches to or relaxations from this central gesture.

## 7. Open Research Threads

- **Key-area maps of Part I** — beyond the Recapitulation, the Coda combines themes; are there sub-keys embedded in the thematic combination that deserve their own map?
- **The role of D major** — why Gretchen's D major? Is there a tonal-hierarchy reason (D-flat major in Part I = sister key), or is it purely a vocal/affective choice?
- **B major at the Mater Gloriosa's first appearance** — what is the relationship between B major (the dominant of E major) and the E major breakthrough key? A prefiguring or an extension?
